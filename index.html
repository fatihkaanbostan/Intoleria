For celiac households

Gluten-free shopping no longer has to be exhausting.

Intoleria is a mobile app that helps households living with a celiac family member find gluten-free products with confidence, build a budget-friendly weekly meal plan and prepare their shopping list.

Problem

A lifelong diet, scattered information and high costs.

Trust

Because of hidden gluten and cross-contamination risk, product labels have to be read carefully. Interpreting warnings such as "may contain traces of gluten" is left to the household.

Cost

Gluten-free alternatives are often more expensive than their regular equivalents and are not available in every supermarket.

Effort

Finding recipes, searching for suitable products, comparing prices and preparing a shopping list are all done in separate tools.

Menu fatigue

Because options are limited, the question "What should I cook today?" becomes a source of stress every day.

Solution

We make the weekly meal and shopping safe, affordable and effortless.

1

Dietary profile

Restrictions such as celiac disease and lactose intolerance; a separate profile for each household member.

2

Recipes and weekly plan

Recipes that fit the profile and a weekly meal plan.

3

Product matching

Recipe ingredients are matched with suitable products. Products are classified as "verified", "not suitable" or "insufficient info"; a product with missing information is not considered safe.

4

List and budget

Shopping list and budget tracking.

Next stages: live price and stock comparison across supermarkets, the most economical basket, automatic cart transfer, at-home product and food waste tracking, restaurant guide. The first app to combine barcode-based inventory checks, virtual fridge stock tracking, and personalized meal planning all in one assistant. Barcode scanning is the future of this product and is planned for upcoming versions.

Value proposition and competitors

Existing solutions focus on one part of the process. We combine all of it for the household.

Product scanning, price comparison and online sales sit in separate places. Our difference is connecting product suitability to the weekly meal and shopping plan.

Behind the scenes

How do we run the business?

1

Product data

Collecting ingredient and allergen declarations, checking sources, and flagging missing or outdated information.

2

Recipes and planning

Curating gluten-free recipes, matching them with profiles, and calculating quantities and costs.

3

Technology

Developing the app, the database and the matching system.

4

Store connections

Gathering product and price information, and building partnerships with supermarkets and suppliers.

5

Support and quality

Reviewing user reports and keeping data transparent.

Partners: food manufacturers, supermarkets, dietitians, celiac associations, technology providers. Resources: product and recipe database, software infrastructure, experts who review food information.

Who is it for?

Families living with a celiac family member.

Primary target

Families with a celiac child or spouse

The person who handles grocery shopping and meal planning in the household. Aged 28–45, lives in Istanbul, uses a smartphone, and pays attention to food spending and product safety.

Secondary target

Newly diagnosed adults

People who need help getting to know gluten-free products and planning their diet.

Early adopters

Istanbul first, then Türkiye

Parents of children with celiac disease, the newly diagnosed, households with more than one dietary restriction, and people who already use online grocery and meal planning tools.

Market data

A real and recurring need.

Objective data

166,614

Number of people diagnosed with celiac disease in Türkiye (end of November 2023). At the end of 2021 this number was 138,230, an increase of about 20% in two years.

Source: Republic of Türkiye Ministry of Health, Health Information Systems data; for 2023, a statement by the Zonguldak Provincial Health Director; for 2021, an NTV news report.

304%

In one study, gluten-free foods were found to be on average this much more expensive than their gluten-containing counterparts. The price data is from 2015–2016.

Source: Mersin University Department of Food Engineering master's thesis (advisor: Prof. Dr. Mahir Turhan).

17.5%

In the same study, this share of the 200 products carrying a gluten-free logo exceeded the 20 mg/kg gluten limit. Verification is needed even for labelled products.

Source: same thesis.

In addition, a study published in 2025 compared the labels of 456 packaged gluten-free and gluten-containing products sold in Türkiye in terms of price and nutritional quality (Kılınç, 2025, J Sci Food Agric, 105(15), 8798–8805).

Hypotheses we will test

Do they want it?

Demand

The person who does the shopping in a celiac household wants to use an app that links product suitability to the weekly plan. Test: survey and campaign interest.

Can we do it?

Product ingredient and label information can be collected and classified with enough accuracy. Test: a manual verification trial with selected products.

Does it create value?

Viability

Users are willing to pay for advanced features. Test: the willingness-to-pay question in the survey and the campaign result.
